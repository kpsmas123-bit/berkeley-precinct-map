About this map

Precinct-level results for every precinct in Alameda County, limited to the contests that appear on essentially every ballot: statewide constitutional offices, U.S. President / Senate / House, state propositions, region-wide district measures, and the 2021 gubernatorial recall.

Local races are deliberately left out. A city council or school board race shown countywide is a mostly-empty map. Berkeley's local contests live on the Berkeley precinct map.

  • Precinct IDs are not stable between elections — each election uses the geometry the county published for it. Results are never joined across elections.
  • Jurisdiction labels come from the city contests a precinct actually votes in, falling back to the county's city outlines. That matters on city borders, where a precinct's polygon can sit inside a neighbouring city while its voters are on the other city's ballot.
  • Shares are votes for a choice over all votes cast in that contest in that precinct (not over ballots cast).
  • Party presidential primaries are shown per party. Only that party's voters received the ballot, so those shares are within-party, not citywide.
  • Party registration comes from the California Statewide Database, the only source publishing it by precinct — the Secretary of State stops at county and district level. It is joined to these precincts by id, not by overlapping shapes. Each snapshot is taken for one election; where none exists yet the nearest earlier one is used and the panel says so.
  • “vs registration” is the one worth looking at. A plain share map is mostly a map of where the Democrats live; this fits a line through every precinct's Democratic-minus- Republican registration margin and colours what is left over — where a choice ran ahead of or behind what that precinct's registration predicts. The legend reports the fit's R². Like “stands out”, a precinct is only coloured if the gap clears 3 points and its own sampling noise.
  • These are precinct patterns, not voter behaviour. A precinct registered 70% Democratic voting 70% for a Democrat tells you nothing about which individuals did what — the classic ecological fallacy. Read it as geography, not as people.
  • “Stands out” flags precincts whose share differs from the countywide share by more than 3 points and beyond normal sampling noise — not merely a big-looking gap.
  • An election still being counted is marked preliminary and built without a certified canvass, from whichever of three sources the county has released. Best is the cast vote record — one anonymised record per ballot, carrying the precinct and every contest on it — which the Registrar provides on request and which maps every big race at once. Failing that, the county's results map feed carries per-precinct votes for just the one or two contests it chooses to map, and its results portal carries countywide totals for every race with no geography at all. The Latest county totals tab lists all of them; the map shows the ones with precinct figures. None of these sources carries per-precinct registration, so turnout is off for those elections, and all are superseded by the certified Statement of Vote.
  • Precincts with no result in a cast vote record. State law (2 CCR 20126) requires a published cast vote record to have the choices stripped from any ballot that could be traced to an individual voter. In a small enough precinct that covers every ballot, so the precinct is shown as no-data rather than as zero votes. The panel says how many.
  • Region-wide district measures (a measure put to the whole county by a district that spans it, such as the 2026 transit measure) are included. Measures from one city, school or park district are not — they would leave the county map mostly empty.
